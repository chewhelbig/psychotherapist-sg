Infidelity Counselling · Singapore

After an Affair

Nothing has to be decided in the first weeks. Most of what gets decided then gets revisited anyway.

The First Weeks

Couples usually come to me within days or weeks of discovery, in a state neither of them recognises. The one who was betrayed cannot stop asking and cannot bear the answers. The one who had the affair is apologising into a space where apology does not land. Both are exhausted and neither is sleeping.

This period has its own character, and it is not the work that follows. It is acute. The betrayed partner is not being unreasonable; they are in something closer to a traumatic state, with the intrusive images and the sudden physical surges that go with it. The other is often frozen, and the freeze gets read as indifference.

Early sessions are mostly about making the pair survivable. Slowing the interrogations. Containing the disclosures. Getting some sleep back. Before anything is asked about the future.

The question underneath the questions

The betrayed partner asks for detail. Where, when, how many times, what did you say to her. The detail is sought because it seems that with enough of it the thing will make sense, and the seeking does not stop when the detail is supplied. What is being asked is usually one question: was I ever the person I thought I was to you.

Part of the work is getting to that question, because it is the one that can be answered.

Both Sides of It

For the Partner Who Had the Affair

Repeated apology does not repair. What repairs, slowly, is being willing to stay present for your partner's pain without defending yourself, on more occasions than seems fair, over a longer period than you expect. Most people can manage this for a fortnight. The work is doing it for months.

There is also your own account to understand—what the affair was for, what it let you feel or avoid. That is not an excuse, and it is not the same as blaming the marriage. It is necessary if anything is to change.

Shame usually arrives here, and it makes staying present harder. It is easier to promise, to perform contrition, or to go quiet, than to sit in front of someone while they look at you.

Deciding whether to stay

You do not have to know. A great many couples come without knowing, and one of the honest uses of therapy is to find out. That is different work from repair, and I will name which one we are doing, so that nobody is quietly pursuing a different project from the other.

Some couples rebuild something better than what preceded it. Some discover the marriage had ended some time before the affair. Both are real outcomes, and neither is the goal I bring into the room.

If you decide to separate

Doing it with some care matters, particularly with children involved. Ending a marriage well is its own piece of work, and it is available here.

Gestalt Couples Therapy

How the Work Goes

Sessions are 80 minutes, usually weekly at the start and less often as things steady. I work in a Gestalt frame, which means the attention is on what is happening between you in the room as much as on the account of what happened outside it. I do not take sides, and I do not treat one of you as the patient. This is the same frame as the rest of my couples counselling work, applied to a particular kind of rupture.

I will also say clearly that I do not hold secrets. If there is ongoing contact with the other person, that will need to come into the room. Work built on a concealed fact does not hold.

Common Questions

Infidelity Counselling in Singapore — FAQ

Should we come together or separately?

Together for the first session. Individual sessions can be useful later, with an agreement in advance about what is confidential and what is not.

Is it too soon to come?

No. The acute period is a reasonable time to start, and often the most useful.

Can this work if the affair is not fully over?

Not well. That is usually the first thing we address.

How long does repair take?

Longer than couples hope. The acute phase is weeks; meaningful repair is generally many months. What shortens it is honesty early rather than a staged disclosure over time.

What if only one of us wants to come?

Individual work is still worth doing, and a relationship changes when one person in it changes. If your partner later wants to join, that can be arranged.

Do you offer online couples sessions?

Yes, including with the two of you in different places. Where it is possible, I would want the first session with you together in the same room.
