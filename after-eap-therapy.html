After Your EAP Sessions

Six sessions is usually where the work starts, not where it finishes.

When the Allocation Runs Out

Employee assistance programmes are useful. They get people into a room who would not otherwise have gone, and for a contained difficulty a few sessions can be enough. Most programmes cover three to eight sessions. Most of what brings people to therapy does not resolve in three to eight sessions.

What usually happens

The pattern is consistent. The first session is history and orientation. By the third you have begun to trust the person opposite you. By the fifth something real is finally on the table. Then the allocation is finished, you are told to consider whether you want to seek further support, and the momentum goes.

People often read this as their own failure—they were given help and did not make it work. The programme did what it was designed to do, which was short-term and capped. The mismatch is structural.

What continuing looks like

You do not start again. In a first session I will ask what you covered, what helped, what you found yourself unable to say, and where it stopped. Work already done is not wasted. It usually means we begin further along than we otherwise would.

You are also free to bring what did not work. Sometimes the useful material is precisely what went wrong with the previous arrangement—the sense of being processed, or of being on a clock.

Privacy

Confidentiality

This is the question most people want to ask and often do not. Under an employer programme, the provider usually reports usage data back to the company. Your content is meant to be protected, but the arrangement is not between you and the therapist alone.

Here it is. You engage me directly and you pay me directly. There is no employer, no platform, and no third party in the room. Nothing goes to your company. Nothing goes to a provider network. I keep clinical notes as I am professionally required to, and they are not shared without your written consent except where the law requires it, which I will explain plainly if you want the detail.

If your programme is still running

You do not have to wait until the sessions run out. Some people run the two in parallel for a short period, or come here for the deeper strand while completing the programme. It is worth telling me if that is the case, so the work does not duplicate or contradict itself.

Gestalt Psychotherapy

How the Work Differs

Short programmes are built around a defined problem and a defined number of meetings. That shape suits some difficulties well. It suits others badly, because the thing that needed attention only became visible once you had stopped performing competence in the room.

My work is Gestalt, and it is open-ended by design. The attention is on what is happening now rather than on progress against a plan. Where an EAP course ended just as something was surfacing—shame, most commonly, or something about a relationship you had not said aloud—this is the register that can hold it.

There is no homework and no deliberate exposure to stress. The work happens in the sessions.

Common Questions

Continuing After EAP — FAQ

Will my employer know I am seeing you?

No. There is no reporting relationship of any kind. Your company has no connection to this practice.

Can you write to my EAP counsellor?

Only with your written consent, and only if you want that. Many people prefer a clean separation.

I only had two sessions and stopped. Does that count?

It counts as information. Whatever made you stop is worth understanding, and often it is the most useful thing you bring.

How many sessions will I need here?

There is no fixed number and no package. Some people come weekly for a period and then stop. Some come once a month. Some return after a year. All of these are legitimate ways to use a practice.

Do I have to repeat everything I already told the last therapist?

No. I will ask where it got to and what mattered. The history comes back in on its own where it is needed.

Is this available online?

Yes. Sessions are in-person in Tiong Bahru or online, and many people use both.
