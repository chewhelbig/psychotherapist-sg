Shame Therapy · Singapore

Shame Therapy in Singapore

The feeling that something is wrong with you, rather than with something you did.

When Shame Brings People to Therapy

Most people who come to me for shame do not call it shame. They call it self-doubt, or being too sensitive, or not knowing why they cannot say the thing they mean to say. Shame rarely announces itself. It arrives dressed as something else.

The difference between shame and guilt

Guilt points at an act. An act can be repaired, and guilt eases when it is—through apology, through amends, through doing it differently next time.

Shame points at the person. It says the fault sits in what you are, and there is nothing there to repair. This is why guilt can be spoken and shame usually cannot. Speaking it seems to confirm it.

The distinction matters in the room because the two ease in different ways. Shame eases in contact. In being seen by someone and not turned away from.

How shame shows itself

Perfectionism is often shame at work. If nothing can be found, nothing can be exposed. So the report is rewritten a fourth time, the message is drafted and deleted, and the standard rises each time it is met.

Withdrawal is another form. You cancel. You reply late, then later, until replying at all has become impossible. From outside it looks like indifference. From inside it is the arithmetic of exposure.

Some people meet shame with anger, sudden and out of proportion to whatever caused it. Others become endlessly accommodating, agreeing before they have decided whether they agree. Both are ways of managing the same intolerable feeling.

In the Room

The Body in Shame

Shame is visible before it is thought. The eyes drop. The chest sinks. Heat rises in the face and neck. There is a wish, quite literal, to be smaller—to take up less room, to be less findable.

In a session I will often notice this before you have named anything. You will be describing something ordinary and your voice will change, or you will look away. That moment is the work. Not the story. The moment.

Where shame is learned

Shame is learned in relationship, usually early, usually without anyone intending it. A parent who withdrew when you were too much. A teacher who made an example of you. A family in which one kind of feeling was welcome and another was not. Children do not conclude that the adult is wrong. They conclude that they are.

It can also be learned late, and fast. Humiliation at work. A relationship in which you were repeatedly made small. Moving to a country where the way you speak marks you out.

Shame in relationships

To be close to someone is to be seen by them, and being seen is what shame cannot survive. So people manage intimacy by staying slightly out of view. They give the good version. They do not ask for what they want, because wanting is itself exposing.

The partner experiences this as distance without a cause. They know something is being withheld and cannot find out what. This is often what brings a couple to couples counselling—one person's shame, quietly organising both of them.

Gestalt Psychotherapy

How I Work with Shame

Shame was made in relationship and it changes in relationship. What matters is less the insight than what happens between us while you say the difficult thing and I do not withdraw.

We begin with awareness. When it arrives, what precedes it, what your body does with it. Then we look at what you do to manage it—the perfectionism, the withdrawal, the pre-emptive apology—and treat those as intelligent responses that once made sense.

In time we work with the relationships in which the shame was learned, and with the way it lives now in your work and your intimate life. This is Gestalt therapy: the attention stays on what is happening between us in the present, rather than on an account of the past delivered from a distance.

My doctoral research is on shame, and the book I am writing is on shame. It is the centre of my work rather than a corner of it.

Why shame is hard to bring to therapy

You may have noticed that you can talk about anxiety, or work, or your family, and there is one subject you circle and never land on. That is usually where the shame is. Shame protects itself, because exposure is the thing it fears.

So I do not go looking for it. It comes into the room on its own, once the room is safe enough.

What Changes

Shame does not disappear. Its authority changes. You begin to recognise it as a feeling that has arrived rather than a fact about who you are, and the gap between the feeling and what you do about it gets wider. In that gap there is a choice.

People describe it afterwards as being able to stay in the room. To say the thing and remain, rather than say it and vanish.

Common Questions

Shame Therapy in Singapore — FAQ

Is this the same as low self-esteem?

They overlap. Self-esteem is an evaluation; shame is an experience. You can hold a fair estimate of your own abilities and still feel, in a particular moment, unfit to be seen. Working at the level of evaluation rarely reaches it.

What is the difference between shame and guilt?

Guilt points at an act, and an act can be repaired. Shame points at the person and says the fault is in what you are. Guilt eases with apology and amends. Shame eases in contact—in being seen by another person and not turned away from.

Do I have to talk about my childhood?

Not at the start, and not ever if it is not useful. We begin with what is happening now. The past becomes relevant when it turns up in the present, which it usually does on its own.

How long does this take?

Longer than symptom-focused work. Relief often comes early, in the first sessions, from the plain fact of having said it aloud. The deeper change, where shame stops organising your relationships, takes months rather than weeks.

How is this different from CBT for self-criticism?

CBT works on the content of self-critical thought and the behaviour that follows it, through structured technique. Gestalt works on what happens in contact—what shame does to your body, and to the relationship in the room, while you are inside it. Shame is regulated between people, so the relational route reaches it more directly.

Can this be done online?

Yes, and it works well. Some people find it easier to begin online, for the reason you would expect.
