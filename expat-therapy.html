Expat Therapy · Singapore

Therapy for Expats in Singapore

The move worked. The rest of it has not settled.

The Arrival That Doesn't Settle

Singapore is easy to arrive in and hard to land in. The logistics go well—the flat, the school, the pass—and then some months later something is wrong that has no obvious cause and no obvious person to tell.

The first year is usually busy enough to carry you. It is the second that exposes things. The novelty has worn off, the friendships are pleasant and shallow, and the life you left has closed over the space where you were. People at home have stopped asking. You have run out of things to say about the weather and the food.

There is often guilt attached to this, because the posting is a good one and the complaint sounds ungrateful. It is not ungrateful. Dislocation is real, and it does not care whether the destination is desirable.

Where it usually shows first

Sleep goes. Or the drinking creeps up, in a city where every social occasion is arranged around it. Or you find yourself unaccountably furious in a supermarket. Symptoms of anxiety often arrive first and get treated as the problem, when they are the surface of a dislocation that has not been spoken about anywhere.

Relocation and the Couple

What a Move Does to a Relationship

A move concentrates a relationship. The usual distributions—friends, family, work, routine—are gone, and the two of you are suddenly carrying all of it for each other. Whatever was already strained becomes the only thing in the room.

Very often the couple has also become unequal in a way it was not before. One person's career is the reason you are here. The other's has been paused, reduced to something smaller, or given up. That imbalance is rarely discussed directly, and it seeps into everything else—money, chores, who gets to be tired.

The accompanying partner

If you followed someone here, you may be living a life you did not exactly choose, in a country you did not exactly pick, with your own work suspended and your days organised around other people's schedules. It is common to feel grateful and erased at the same time.

This is one of the most frequent reasons expats come to therapy in Singapore, and one of the least frequently named in a first session.

Children, school, and the question of home

Children often adapt faster than their parents, and in ways that unsettle them. Accents that are not yours. A home that is here rather than where you are from. A sense that the family's story has changed without anyone agreeing to it. Parents can feel obscurely bereaved about this and find it hard to say so.

Where the strain sits between the two of you rather than inside one of you, couples counselling is usually the more direct route.

When the posting ends

Repatriation is often harder than the move out, and almost nobody prepares for it. You return to a place that has not kept your seat, changed in ways that are difficult to explain to people who did not go. Some couples discover that what held them together was the shared project of being abroad.

How I Work

Working Across Difference

I trained in Vienna and worked for some years with asylum seekers, which is where I learned most of what I know about contact across difference. How to sit with someone whose reference points are not mine without pretending to share them, and without treating them as exotic. I am Singaporean, and I have lived and worked elsewhere. Both matter in this work.

The approach is Gestalt. The attention stays on what is happening now—in your body, in the room, in the way you describe a place you no longer live in.

In person and online

The practice is at 65 Tiong Poh Road in Tiong Bahru, which is central and easy to reach. Sessions can also be online, and many clients use both: in person when they are here, online when work takes them elsewhere. If you leave Singapore, the work can continue without interruption, subject to the licensing rules of wherever you land, which we can check together.

Common Questions

Expat Therapy in Singapore — FAQ

Do you work with people who are only here for a year or two?

Yes, and it is worth doing even on a short horizon. We can work to a defined ending from the outset, which changes the shape of the work rather than diminishing it.

My partner won't come. Is individual work still useful?

Yes. A relationship changes when one person in it changes, and there is a great deal to work with on your own. If your partner later wants to join, that can be arranged.

Can we do couples sessions when one of us is travelling?

Yes. Couples sessions work online with both of you in different places, though I would want the first session with you together in the same room where that is possible.

What happens if I leave Singapore mid-way through?

The work continues online. The one thing to check is the licensing position of the country you are moving to, which we can look at together before you go.

What language do you work in?

English.
