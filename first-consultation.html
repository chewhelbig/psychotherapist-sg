Starting Therapy · Singapore

The First Consultation

A consultation, not a commitment.

What You Are Agreeing To

Booking a first session is the part most people find hardest, and it is usually harder than the session itself. So it is worth saying plainly what you are agreeing to, which is one meeting.

What happens in the fifty minutes

We talk. I will ask what brought you now—not what brought you generally, but why this month rather than last year, because the timing is usually informative. I will ask something about your history, your work, and the people close to you, though not exhaustively. There is no intake questionnaire to get through.

Mostly I will be paying attention to what happens while you speak. Where you slow down. What you skirt. What changes in your body when a particular name comes up. That is the material of the work, and it is available from the first session.

You do not need to have prepared anything, and you do not need to know what your problem is. Not knowing is a common and workable starting point.

What you might want to ask me

You are entitled to ask how I work, what my training is, whether I have worked with what you are bringing, and what you should expect. You are also entitled to ask the awkward question—whether you are making too much of this. People ask it often. The answer is almost always no.

Fit

If It Is Not a Fit

Sometimes it is not, and that is a legitimate outcome of a first meeting rather than a failure of one. If I think someone else would serve you better—a psychiatrist, a specialist in an area I do not work in, or simply a different person—I will say so and help you find them. You are under no obligation to book again, and I will not follow up to ask.

Coming back after a long gap

If you have seen me before, you do not need a reason to return and you do not need to explain the gap. Some people use this practice continuously for a period, some come for a few sessions when something needs attention, and some reappear after several years. All three are normal. You have a therapist. That does not lapse.

Common Questions

First Consultation — FAQ

Do I have to commit to weekly sessions?

No. Weekly suits some work and some periods. Fortnightly and monthly are both workable, and we can decide together after the first meeting rather than before it.

What if I cry, or cannot speak?

Both happen and neither is a problem. Silence in a session is not wasted time.

Can I bring my partner to the first session?

You can, but decide which you are booking. A couples session is a different piece of work with a different fee and length, and it is worth being clear which one you want.

Is what I say confidential?

Yes, with the standard limits, which I will set out at the start—essentially, situations involving serious risk to you or someone else, or a legal requirement. Nothing is shared with an employer, insurer or family member without your written consent.

Do I need a referral?

No. You can book directly.

How do I know if it is working?

Early on, usually by something small. Sleeping. Saying a thing you had been carrying. Noticing a pattern while you are inside it rather than a week later.
