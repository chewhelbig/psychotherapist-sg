Therapy Groups · Singapore

Therapy groups that meet
in the evening

Small closed groups of four to eight people, ninety minutes, at Tiong Bahru. Each group has a theme and runs as a fixed block, so the same people are in the room each time.

A group gives you something individual therapy cannot: other people who recognise it. Not advice, and not a workshop — a room where what you have been carrying alone turns out to be shared, and where you get to see how you are with others while it is happening.

A group only runs if at least four people book. If a group does not reach four by its closing date, it does not run and your deposit is returned in full.

How Booking Works

Your Deposit Is Only Ever at Risk of Being Returned

  1. You reserve a place with a $120 deposit. Paid by card or PayNow. This holds one of the four to eight places and counts towards the minimum.
  2. We have a short conversation. Fifteen minutes by phone or video, within a few days. Group work suits most people, but not everyone and not at every moment. If either of us thinks it is not right, your deposit is returned in full.
  3. On the closing date, the group is confirmed or it is not. Four or more places filled and the group runs — you get the dates, the venue details, the group agreement, and a link to pay the balance. Fewer than four and the group does not run: your deposit goes back to where it came from, automatically, and you owe nothing.
  4. The balance is due before the first session. $560 for a six-session block, $240 for a three-session block. Nobody is asked for the balance until their group is confirmed.

Once a group is running, the block is the commitment. A closed group depends on the same people being in the room, so sessions are not transferable or refundable after the first session, and missed sessions cannot be made up individually. This is what makes the group work rather than a rule for its own sake.

Common Questions

Questions About Group Therapy

What actually happens in a session?

We sit in a circle. There is no curriculum and no going round the room in turn. People bring what is live for them, and the work happens in what passes between us — what someone says, what it lands on in someone else, what you notice happening in yourself while you listen. I keep the room safe and workable and bring attention to what is going on, including the things nobody has said out loud yet. Ninety minutes moves faster than you expect.

Do I have to talk?

Not at any particular moment, and not before you are ready. Most people say very little in the first session and are surprised by how much they say by the third. Being in the room and listening is participation. What does not work is coming and staying permanently outside it, and if that is what is happening I will say so, privately.

What if the group does not fill?

Then it does not run, and your deposit is refunded in full to the card or account you paid from. This usually takes five to ten working days to appear, depending on your bank. You are never charged the balance for a group that does not run, and you do not have to chase me for it — the refund is issued automatically on the closing date.

Why is there a minimum of four?

Below four it stops being a group. With three people the room narrows quickly, one absence changes everything, and the thing that makes group work useful — the range of other people in it — is not there. Eight is the ceiling for the opposite reason: past that, ninety minutes cannot hold everyone.

Is what I say in the group confidential?

Everyone signs a group agreement before the first session, and what is said in the room stays in the room. My own professional confidentiality obligations apply exactly as they do in individual therapy, with the same legal limits where there is risk of serious harm. Group confidentiality does depend on the members as well as on me, which is part of why there is a screening conversation.

Can I do a group and individual therapy at the same time?

Yes, and many people do. The two reach different things — individual work goes deeper into your own material, group work shows you what you do with other people while you are doing it. If you already see another therapist, that is fine; I would just suggest telling them you are joining a group.

Could I run into someone I know?

Singapore is small, so it is worth thinking about. In the screening conversation I will ask whether there is anyone you would not want to be in a group with, and I will not place two people together where there is an existing connection. If it happens by chance at the first session, either of you can withdraw with a full refund.

What if I have to miss a session?

Come to the ones you can. The block is priced as a whole and missed sessions are not refunded or carried over, because your place is held either way and the group is shaped around a fixed membership. If something happens that means you cannot continue at all, talk to me.

Is a group cheaper than individual therapy?

Per session, yes — $120 for ninety minutes in a group compared with $220 for fifty minutes individually. But it is not a discounted version of individual therapy. It does a different job, and for some things it is the better instrument rather than the cheaper one.

Who should not join a group right now?

Group work is not the right first step if you are in acute crisis, if you are actively at risk, or if you are in the early weeks after a major trauma. That is not a judgement about you — it is about what a group can hold. In those situations individual work first is the better route, and we can talk about that in the screening call.
